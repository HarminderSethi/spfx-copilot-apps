# When Agents Get a UX

## A 10-video global campaign for business and technology decision makers

## The big idea

For years, digital work has meant knowing where to go.

Which app? Which portal? Which dashboard? Which form? Which system owns the next step?

Copilot changes the starting point. People can simply say what they need. The agent understands the intent. And now, with Copilot Apps, an agent can do something far more powerful than return another paragraph: it can host and render the right experience for that moment inside Copilot.

A form when the person needs to act. A comparison when they need to decide. A dashboard when they need context. A confirmation when the action matters. Different intent, different UX - selected dynamically by the agent.

The campaign has one message:

> **AI understands the request. The UX component makes it precise. The user remains in control.**

This is the art of the possible when AI and deterministic operations work together:

1. **Probabilistic conversational understanding** interprets what the person means and extracts the details they actually provided.
2. **Deterministic UX and operations** make those details visible, apply trusted business rules, and require deliberate review before consequential action.

The agent becomes a secure UX host inside Copilot. Copilot provides the canvas and user context; the experience can adapt to intent while existing identity, access, and security boundaries continue to matter. Existing business solutions can remain the systems of record. What changes is how naturally people reach and use them.

HR is the opening showcase because one employee journey can demonstrate forms, calculations, visual explanations, confirmations, and immersive workspaces in seconds. It is proof of the model, not the boundary of the story. The same capability applies to sales, operations, governance, finance, customer service, knowledge work, and every existing solution that needs a better experience in the flow of work.

## The campaign ambition

This is not a product-feature campaign. It is a possibility campaign.

The audience should finish each video thinking:

- **“Wait. Copilot can do that?”**
- **“Why are we still sending people to another portal for this?”**
- **“Which of our business experiences should an agent bring into Copilot?”**

The purpose is not to explain how Copilot Apps are built. The purpose is to make leaders see a new interaction model for their organization.

## Audience

### Business decision makers

The business audience should see:

- Fewer handoffs and less app switching.
- Higher completion and adoption because the right UX appears at the moment of need.
- Better trust because people can see exactly what will happen.
- Faster modernization without forcing every journey into one generic chatbot pattern.
- Agents that can host the appropriate UX across employee, customer, manager, governance, operational, and executive work.

### Technology decision makers

The technology audience should see:

- Existing solutions and systems of record can power agent-hosted UX inside Copilot.
- Identity, permissions, and security context remain central to the interaction.
- Deterministic rules and operations can sit behind a natural conversational entry point.
- Agents can render rich, automatically hosted experiences in Copilot, reducing the need to send users elsewhere.
- The organization can modernize journey by journey instead of replacing everything at once.

## The storytelling formula

Every short uses the same memorable three-beat structure.

### Beat 1: Understand

A person describes the outcome they need in natural language.

**Narrative:** “I should not need to know the system. I should only need to know my intent.”

### Beat 2: Make precise

The agent selects and renders the right UX component inside Copilot. The person can see the fields, evidence, calculations, options, or visual explanation appropriate to their intent.

**Narrative:** “The request becomes specific enough to trust.”

### Beat 3: Keep control

The person reviews, adjusts, filters, chooses, or confirms. Copilot does not blur the line between understanding and authorization.

**Narrative:** “AI gets me there. I decide what happens.”

## Business value pillars

## In the flow of work

The experience appears where the conversation already happens. People do not need to understand the application landscape before starting.

## Existing solutions, modernized

Copilot Apps can bring the value of existing systems, data, workflows, and business rules into a new conversational front door. Modernization does not require abandoning the systems that already run the business.

## Intent-adaptive UX

One agent does not need one static interface. It can select different UX components for different intents: a form for action, a chart for explanation, a comparison for choice, or a dashboard for context.

## Automatically hosted in Copilot

The agent presents the selected UX in Copilot, inline or full screen, without sending the user through another disconnected journey.

## Security and identity remain central

The experience can honor the signed-in person, existing access, and organizational controls. The campaign should communicate security as a native part of the experience, not as a technical footnote.

## Deterministic where it matters

Business calculations, evidence, filters, validations, and writes follow trusted operations. AI interprets the request; it does not replace the business rule.

## Human control at the action boundary

## Creative direction

## Tone

- Aspirational, human, and confident.
- Clear enough for a worldwide business audience.
- Never breathless or magical; the “wow” comes from what the viewer sees.
- Avoid acronyms, implementation details, and specialist vocabulary.
- Present control and security as part of great UX, not as caveats.

## Opening frames

- Begin with recognizable friction: app switching, searching, spreadsheets, portals, or disconnected approvals.
- Show the natural-language request by second four.
- Let the Copilot-hosted UX transformation happen on screen.
- Do not begin with a behind-the-scenes diagram or a long logo animation.

## Motion

- Keep the prompt-to-UX transition in one continuous shot whenever possible.
- Use one push-in for the inline experience and one wider reveal for full screen.
- Pause on calculations, evidence, and review states long enough to understand them.
- Show the cursor only when the user is about to make a deliberate choice.
- Never add a success sound before confirmation.

- Burn in captions on every short.
- Keep captions to two lines and use plain global English.
- Highlight one campaign word per beat: **UNDERSTANDS**, **PRECISE**, **CONTROL**.

- Create subtitle files for accessibility and localization.

## Truth and trust principles

The campaign can be visionary without overclaiming.

## Always communicate

- AI interprets the request; trusted operations produce the deterministic result.
- The UX exposes important values and evidence.
- The person reviews consequential actions.
- The agent hosts intent-adaptive UX inside Copilot, in the flow of work.
- Identity, permissions, and security remain part of the experience.
- Existing business systems can remain behind the experience.

## Never imply

- AI silently approved or submitted a consequential action.
- Security or permissions were bypassed for convenience.
- A simulated value is live production data.
- Every organization receives the experience without integration to its existing solutions.
- Conversational understanding replaces business rules or accountability.

## Modernization workshop follow-through

The campaign CTA should lead naturally into a business workshop, not a technical download.

## 60-minute workshop outline

1. **Map friction:** Where do people leave the flow of work?
2. **Choose intent:** What would they naturally ask Copilot?
3. **Reveal precision:** What UX, evidence, or calculation must appear?
4. **Define control:** What must the user review or confirm?
5. **Respect trust:** Which identity, security, compliance, and data boundaries apply?
6. **Connect the present:** Which existing solution remains the system of record?
7. **Select the first journey:** Which scenario can prove value quickly and visibly?

- One priority journey.
- One natural-language starting request.
